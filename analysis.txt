
**The most likely cause is in the client, not the server tick.** In `sim-race-webgl.html`, the "me" block of `applyNet()` pulls your own character back onto stale server positions. The screen shows you behind where the server really has you. You release with room to spare, then the character slides forward over the edge.

**Evidence**
- In your clip, the stick returns to centre and the character leaves the platform about 0.3 s later.
- I replayed your own correction formulas with `sim.js` and a 30 Hz server in a headless test. The on-screen player lags pure prediction by 0.9 units at 200 ms ping, 2.4 at 400 ms and 5.3 at 800 ms. After release it slides forward by the same amount.
- When the test player released with 1 unit of visible margin, it fell 20/20 times at 400 and 800 ms. At 200 ms with ±40 ms jitter it fell 8/20 times.

**Why**
- The correction target is the last snapshot plus its velocity times `age`. `age` only counts time since the snapshot arrived, but the snapshot reflects inputs a full round trip old. The correction (rate 10/s) drags you onto that stale spot.
- The `wInp` gate only protects about `rtt/2 + 100 ms` after an input change. It doesn't cover the full round trip or a steady hold.
- The `corr ≈ 0.2 u` figure in `TODO-netcode.md` can't catch this, because the correction shrinks the error by dragging the screen position.

**Fix I tested (x/z only):** extrapolate the target by time since arrival plus RTT, and gate horizontal correction for RTT plus one tick after each input change. I left the vertical logic alone. Lag dropped to about 0.2 units, the slide disappeared, and falls went to 0/20 at 400 and 800 ms. The jump arc was unchanged (0.16 u at 200 ms). The remaining 5/20 falls at 400 ms ±80 ms are probably jitter. The server applies each input the moment it arrives, with no timestamps or sequence/ack. Fixing that is a bigger change.
